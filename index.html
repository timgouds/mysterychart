Five charts a day, all built from live public data. Everything is there except the one label that tells you what you're looking at.

  1. Read the evidence. Country names, years and numbers all stay visible. Only the subject is hidden, so the answer is there to be worked out rather than guessed.
  2. It gets harder as you go. Chart one offers four options. Chart two, five. By the last chart there are eight, and the wrong ones are chosen to be tempting.
  3. Wrong answers buy hints. A clue appears just under the chart, starting cryptic and ending obvious. You can also ask for one outright. Either way it costs the same.
  4. The harder charts are worth more. Naming a chart straight away scores 100 on the first, and triple that on the last. Every mistake or hint costs you. All five first time earns a bonus, for 1,200 out of a possible 1,000. There's no clock: this rewards reading carefully, not clicking fast.
